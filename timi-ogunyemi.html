Biography

Oluwatimilehin is the Founding and Managing Partner of Timi Ogunyemi & Co. He is a corporate and commercial lawyer with experience spanning banking and finance, regulatory compliance, dispute resolution, taxation, corporate governance, fintech, commercial transactions, and immigration law.

He has advised financial institutions, companies, entrepreneurs, and individuals on commercial transactions, contractual arrangements, regulatory obligations, corporate governance, legal risk, and dispute resolution. His transactional experience includes drafting, reviewing, and negotiating a broad range of commercial agreements and advising clients on the legal and regulatory implications of their business relationships.

Oluwatimilehin has also handled complex banking and financial-services matters involving litigation, arbitration, court orders, account restrictions, regulatory investigations, and claims arising from banking relationships. His experience includes working with internal business teams, regulators, external counsel, and other stakeholders to identify legal risks and develop commercially practical solutions.

His corporate and commercial practice is complemented by experience in regulatory compliance, fintech, taxation, and U.S. immigration law. He has worked on immigration matters involving VAWA self-petitions, T Visas, U Visas, Forms I-360 and I-485, including the preparation of detailed legal arguments and responses to Requests for Evidence.

He brings a commercially focused approach to legal practice, combining careful legal analysis with an understanding of the commercial, regulatory, and operational considerations affecting clients.

Immigration Law

  • Worked with a United States immigration law firm on U.S. immigration matters, with particular responsibility for preparing substantive responses to Requests for Evidence (RFEs) issued by the United States Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS).

  • Contributed to immigration matters resulting in clients obtaining immigration benefits, including lawful permanent resident status, subject to USCIS adjudication.
